# Major brands 1,000 v2

As of 2026-09-19, all **1,000 identities have approved artwork**, up from 812 at the start of the completion effort. Icon and company-name logo coverage are counted separately: covering an identity does not mean every role, layout, or background variant is available. See the [completion report](../../reports/logo-library-completion-2026-09-19/README.md) for the final counts and remaining role list.

`sources.json` is the reproducible, exactly 1,000-identity roster. It is a usage-oriented global mix, **not** a ranked list of the 1,000 largest companies by market capitalization or revenue. It preserves the 100 reviewed v1 identities, adds the repository's major-brand fixture, and selects across researched public-company, consumer, and technology candidate pools. Some entries are prominent product or subsidiary brands rather than separate legal companies; `identityType`, `parentBrandId`, and `externalParentBrandId` make that distinction explicit. The cohort pools and `selection-audit.json` retain the selection trail and exclusions.

`manifest.json` is the approved asset entrypoint. Every selected identity has a row, but an empty `assets` object means that no artwork has passed review. The 100 v1 rows reference immutable assets under `../v1/`; new approved files are content-addressed under `assets/`. Do not infer asset approval or currentness from a file's presence in `assets/`: collection candidates remain in timestamped `runs/` until visual review and explicit approval. `review/staged/<run-id>/` contains light/dark contact sheets for each staged run; `review/` contains the approved library sheets.

The marks remain the property of their respective owners. Asset approval here means identity/provenance/visual review, not a license or permission to use a mark in a product, advertisement, or redistribution. Known usage restrictions remain in `rights-notes.json` and per-brand notes, including for approved public artwork.

`yoink`, the CLI, and the app/API now check the approved library for an exact domain or explicit alias before live discovery. Missing requested roles or preferences still use live discovery; already approved roles survive a live failure. Use `library: false` or CLI `--live` to bypass the library. Runtime packaging includes only approved assets and is rebuilt by `npm run brands:runtime` (also run before packaging and during the deployment build).

Company-name logos include horizontal, stacked, and compact marks. Stacked and compact marks are labeled in `representation` and are not returned as legacy `wide` selections. Theme labels describe the background an asset works on. Native-size favicons and intentional brand monograms can be valid icons; generic UI icons, photos, and other brands' marks are rejected.

Run `node scripts/brand-library.mjs coverage --root brand-library/v2` for the current summary, or add `--output <path>` for the per-brand inventory. It reads the manifest and committed research even when local collection runs are absent. Historical research statuses do not determine current asset coverage.

Maintenance is **manual, whenever you choose**. Follow the [manual refresh procedure](../../docs/manual-logo-refresh.md) for full-library or selected-brand checks, exact-run review and approval, retained artwork on failure, and packaging/deployment steps. It includes a ready-to-paste request for Codex. Routine refreshes preserve the existing roster; the roster build/bootstrap commands are not required. `node scripts/brand-library.mjs verify --root brand-library/v2` validates roster size, uniqueness, parent references, manifest scope, hashes, and provenance.

The review renderer uses transparent `contain` padding. Its earlier opaque padding generated false black bars in contact sheets; affected approvals were rechecked with corrected sheets. `withdraw` and `restore` record secondary-review decisions in `approval-history/` without deleting the original immutable files.

For unresolved identities, research results can be staged with `node scripts/brand-library-stage-deep-search.mjs --root brand-library/v2 --input <results.json>`. This fetches direct candidate artwork into immutable paths and creates a new run report. Then use `review --run <run-id> --compact` to inspect just that run's identities on both backgrounds, followed by curated decisions and explicit approval. A discovered URL is never an automatic approval.

The historical second-pass research audit is in `deep-search/part-a.json`, `part-b.json`, and `part-c.json` (99 non-overlapping identities each). Together they recorded 141 candidate identities, 12 permission-gated identities, and 144 where no suitable direct artwork was found at that time. Candidate status means a source was located, not that its art was approved; several were rejected after visual review or could not be decoded. The approved manifest and `coverage` command are the authority for current usable coverage. Later recovery reviewed public artwork while retaining usage restrictions as notes.

`deep-search/google-images-first-20.json` records the first 20 still-missing identities tested with Google Images. Seven gained approved art (Snowflake, Lowe's, Uniqlo, Patagonia, Ray-Ban, Sephora, and Mondelēz International); three were permission-gated, one promising Commons file was rate-limited, and nine yielded no sufficiently traceable, identity-correct asset. These results show that image search can recover logos, but its hits still require provenance and visual review.
